## Client instructions (file note of conference, 14/04/2026)

Our client, Ms Eleanor Harper (born 1989, schoolteacher of Penrith NSW),
attended Glenbrook Adventure Park at Glenbrook NSW on 22/02/2026 with a
school group. While descending the "Cascade" elevated rope walk she fell
approximately 2.4 metres when a rope guardrail gave way at a turnbuckle
connection point. She landed on compacted gravel and sustained a fractured
left distal radius requiring open reduction and internal fixation at Nepean
Hospital on 23/02/2026, plus soft-tissue injuries to the left hip.

Ms Harper reports the turnbuckle was visibly corroded after the fall. A
park employee, Mr Daniel Okafor, told her at the scene that "that section
was meant to be re-rigged last month". She photographed the failed fitting
(photos held on file). She signed the park's standard entry waiver at the
kiosk on arrival; she does not recall any verbal warnings. A laminated sign
at the rope walk entry read "Participants climb at their own risk".

She has been off work since the incident and returned on graduated duties
on 06/04/2026. Out-of-pocket expenses to date are approximately $14,800
(surgical gap, physiotherapy, travel). She seeks compensation for her
injuries and losses.

## Preliminary advice already given (memo of 02/04/2026)

Counsel's preliminary memo on file notes the following framework. The
foreseeability calculus for breach remains that stated in Wyong Shire
Council v Shirt (1980) 146 CLR 40: a risk that is not far-fetched or
fanciful is foreseeable, and the response is judged by the calculus of
negligence. Causation is approached on a common-sense basis as explained in
March v Stramare (E & MH) Pty Ltd (1991) 171 CLR 506, now refracted through
the statutory test. The neighbour principle traces to Donoghue v Stevenson
[1932] AC 562. On obvious risk and recreational activities, the memo flags
Roads and Traffic Authority of NSW v Dederer [2007] HCA 42 for the
proposition that the duty is to take reasonable care, not to prevent all
harm, and that obviousness of risk informs but does not negate the duty.

The memo identifies the Civil Liability Act 2002 (NSW) as the governing
statute: s 5B (general principles of breach), s 5D (causation), s 5F-5H
(obvious risk), s 5K-5N (recreational activities and risk warnings,
including the effect of a signed waiver for dangerous recreational
activities), and s 50 (intoxication - not applicable here).

## Opposing position

The park's letter of 18/03/2026 asserts: (a) the signed waiver excludes
liability; (b) the rope walk is a dangerous recreational activity and the
fall was the materialisation of an obvious risk; (c) the warning sign
satisfied any duty owed. No without-prejudice offer has been made.

## Instructions sought

Advise on and pursue recovery of damages for personal injury against the
park operator. The client prefers negotiated resolution but will litigate
in the District Court of NSW if required. Limitation: three years from
date of injury under the Limitation Act 1969 (NSW) as modified for personal
injury claims; no urgency but proceedings should be commenced well before
22/02/2029.
