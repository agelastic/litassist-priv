## Issues Found during Verification

1. **“WITHOUT PREJUDICE EXCEPT AS TO COSTS” was inappropriate for the whole letter.** The letter contains notice allegations, an evidence-preservation request and potential reliance for costs. Marking the whole letter without prejudice may undermine later reliance. Corrected to an open letter with any genuine compromise offer to be separately marked if required. **Confidence: HIGH**

2. **Civil Procedure Act 2005 (NSW) pre-action obligations were overstated.** NSW generally does not impose a universal pre-action protocol for ordinary personal injury claims of this type. The overriding purpose applies to civil proceedings and participants once proceedings are on foot. Corrected to say the letter is intended to facilitate early resolution and that any proceedings will be subject to the Act. **Confidence: HIGH**

3. **Civil Liability Act 2002 (NSW) s 5B was described as creating the duty of care.** Section 5B concerns breach of duty; the duty is primarily common law, with breach assessed under the Civil Liability Act. Corrected. **Confidence: HIGH**

4. **Treatment of recreational-activity defences and waivers was too categorical.** The original text stated liability was not excluded by the waiver or ss 5K–5N because the defect was latent. That is a plausible argument but depends on the waiver wording, risk warning, activity characterisation, obviousness and statutory operation, including any Commonwealth consumer-law interaction. Corrected to a more defensible preliminary position. **Confidence: HIGH**

5. **Commonwealth law relevant to recreational-service waivers was omitted.** A commercial adventure park waiver may engage the Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (Cth), including s 139A, as well as NSW law. Corrected by requesting particulars of reliance on any waiver under NSW and Commonwealth law. **Confidence: HIGH**

6. **General damages terminology required NSW Civil Liability Act qualification.** NSW personal injury non-economic loss is subject to Part 2 of the Civil Liability Act 2002 (NSW), including the statutory threshold and scale. Corrected. **Confidence: HIGH**

7. **Indemnity costs were overstated as a consequence of proceedings.** Indemnity costs are discretionary and generally require a proper basis, such as unreasonable rejection of a reasonable offer. Corrected. **Confidence: HIGH**

8. **The evidence-preservation paragraph incorrectly relied on the Evidence Act 1995 (NSW) as creating a general pre-litigation production duty.** The Evidence Act mainly governs admissibility and proof. A prospective litigant should preserve evidence once litigation is reasonably contemplated, but voluntary pre-action production is generally a request unless a court order or subpoena/discovery process applies. Corrected. **Confidence: HIGH**

9. **Demand for immediate production of employee statements and internal investigation documents was too absolute.** Such documents may be subject to legal professional privilege or other objections. Corrected to request voluntary production of non-privileged documents and identification of any privilege claim. **Confidence: HIGH**

10. **Threat to commence in the District Court of New South Wales without quantified damages was slightly premature.** The District Court is likely appropriate for many personal injury claims, but the final forum depends on quantum and jurisdictional limits. Corrected to “appropriate NSW court, presently anticipated to be the District Court of New South Wales”. **Confidence: MEDIUM**

11. **Strategic reasoning overstated verification of facts.** It referred to facts as “expressly verified in the supporting document”, but the provided material does not independently verify all facts. Corrected to “facts as presently instructed”. **Confidence: HIGH**

12. **“Counsel contacts” in the mediation paragraph was an assumption.** Counsel may not yet be retained. Corrected to insurer, claims manager and legal representative contacts. **Confidence: HIGH**
