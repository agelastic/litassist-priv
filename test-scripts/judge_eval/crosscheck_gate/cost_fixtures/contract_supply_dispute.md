# Advice Memo - Breach of Supply Agreement

**Matter:** Brightwater Cafes Pty Ltd v Nardone Coffee Supplies Pty Ltd
**Jurisdiction:** New South Wales
**Prepared:** for the instructing solicitor

## Background

Brightwater Cafes Pty Ltd (ACN 123 456 789) operates a chain of seven cafes in
Sydney. On 12 January 2025 it entered a two-year exclusive supply agreement with
Nardone Coffee Supplies Pty Ltd for the supply of roasted coffee beans at a fixed
price of $28 per kilogram, with monthly deliveries of not less than 400 kilograms.

From March 2025 Nardone made three consecutive short deliveries (260kg, 180kg and
nil) and on 2 May 2025 purported to increase the price to $41 per kilogram, citing
"unforeseen green bean cost increases". Brightwater was forced to source
replacement stock on the spot market at an average of $46 per kilogram for two
months, incurring approximately $38,000 in additional cost, and lost an estimated
$15,000 in margin from reduced menu availability.

## Issues

1. Whether Nardone's conduct constitutes a repudiation or breach of an essential
   term entitling Brightwater to terminate and claim damages.
2. Whether the purported price variation is effective absent a variation clause.
3. The measure of damages, including mitigation.

## Analysis

A supplier's persistent failure to deliver contracted quantities, culminating in a
nil delivery, is capable of amounting to repudiation where it evinces an intention
no longer to be bound by the contract: *Koompahtoo Local Aboriginal Land Council v
Sanpine Pty Ltd* (2007) 233 CLR 115. The fixed-price term appears to be a
condition; a unilateral 46 per cent price increase without any contractual
variation mechanism is not effective and is itself a breach.

Damages are assessed on the expectation basis to put Brightwater in the position
it would have occupied had the contract been performed: *Robinson v Harman* (1848)
1 Ex 850. The additional spot-market cost and lost margin are recoverable subject
to the duty to mitigate, which Brightwater appears to have discharged by sourcing
replacement stock promptly. Consequential losses must not be too remote: *Hadley v
Baxendale* (1854) 9 Exch 341.

## Conclusion

Brightwater has strong prospects of establishing repudiation and recovering the
additional acquisition cost and lost margin. A letter of demand should issue
before proceedings in the District Court of New South Wales, which has
jurisdiction for the quantum involved.
