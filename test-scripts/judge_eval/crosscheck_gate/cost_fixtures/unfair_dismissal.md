# Advice Memo - Unfair Dismissal

**Matter:** Re Priya Anand - termination by Meridian Logistics Pty Ltd
**Jurisdiction:** Federal (Fair Work) - employee resident in Victoria
**Prepared:** for the instructing solicitor

## Background

Ms Priya Anand was employed by Meridian Logistics Pty Ltd as a warehouse
supervisor from 4 February 2019 until her dismissal on 28 April 2025. Meridian
employs about 90 staff and is not a small business employer. Ms Anand earned
$84,000 per annum, below the high income threshold.

On 24 April 2025 a pallet of stock was found damaged. The site manager accused Ms
Anand of negligence, did not put the specific allegation to her in writing, held a
single ten-minute meeting at which she was not permitted a support person, and
terminated her four days later by email. Ms Anand says two other supervisors had
access to the area and that no investigation of the forklift logs was undertaken.

## Issues

1. Whether the dismissal was harsh, unjust or unreasonable under s 387 of the Fair
   Work Act 2009 (Cth).
2. Whether procedural fairness was afforded (notice of reason, opportunity to
   respond, support person).
3. Remedies: reinstatement or compensation, and the compensation cap.

## Analysis

The Commission must consider whether there was a valid reason for the dismissal
related to capacity or conduct, and the procedural matters in s 387(b)-(h). A
single short meeting, without the allegation being particularised and without the
opportunity to have a support person, weighs against the employer. The failure to
examine the forklift logs suggests the reason may not be sound, defensible or well
founded: *Selvachandran v Peteron Plastics Pty Ltd* (1995) 62 IR 371.

Even where a valid reason exists, a dismissal may still be unfair if the process
was deficient. Reinstatement is the primary remedy under s 390; compensation is
capped at the lesser of 26 weeks' pay or half the high income threshold.

## Conclusion

Ms Anand has reasonable prospects in an unfair dismissal application. The
application must be lodged within 21 days of the dismissal taking effect under s
394. Given the timeline, urgent instructions on remedy preference are required.
