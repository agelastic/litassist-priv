The defendant's primary defences present greater complexity: the "obvious risk" and "dangerous recreational activity" provisions under ss 5K–5N may be argued, but the risk that materialised (a corroded fitting that had allegedly been deferred for re-rigging) may be distinguished from the general risk of participation in a rope walk. The waiver under s 5N, and any relevant Commonwealth consumer law recreational-services exclusion, must be assessed against the actual wording and circumstances of the waiver; enforceability in the face of an alleged latent equipment defect and known maintenance failure will be a key battleground. Mr Okafor's alleged admission is significant if admissible and accepted, because it may undermine the "obvious risk" and "waiver" defences and support proof of actual or constructive knowledge of a maintenance issue.

The defendant's defences are arguable but may be weakened by evidence of known deferred maintenance and visible corrosion, which suggest this was potentially a latent equipment defect rather than merely an obvious risk of the recreational activity itself.
