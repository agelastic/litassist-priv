## A. Leaderboard credibility note — discount, don't trust

### A.1 What the leaderboard says

"Updated June 27, 2026. 76 models reviewed, 19 benchmarks. Best Overall: Claude Fable 5 (Anthropic). Best Value: MiniMax M2.1. Best Open Weights: MiniMax M2.1. Longest Context: Qwen3.7 Max." Named benchmarks: **LegalBench** (diverse reasoning tasks), **Uniform Bar Exam (MBE + essay)**, **contract analysis accuracy**. The remaining 16 of "19 benchmarks" are not enumerated.

Top six listed: Qwen3.7 Max → MiniMax M2.1 → Claude Opus 4.8 → Qwen3.6 Plus → Claude Opus 4.7 → Kimi K2.5.

### A.2 What it actually measures

Of the three benchmarks named, **all three are US-jurisdiction-coded**:
- **LegalBench** (Guha et al. 2023, Stanford CRFM): 162 tasks, the vast majority US federal / state law. Predominantly multiple-choice. Known prompt-sensitive.
- **Uniform Bar Exam (MBE + essay)**: the US bar exam, 50-state common content. No AU equivalent component.
- **Contract analysis accuracy**: typically US/English-law contract templates (CUAD dataset and similar).

For LitAssist's actual workload — Australian medium-neutral citations, AGLC4 formatting, NSW vs Vic vs Cth jurisdictional reasoning, AustLII case-law lookup — these benchmarks have very low ecological validity. A model that wins on UBE multiple-choice can still confidently cite a non-existent NSWCA decision.

### A.3 Methodology concerns

1. **The 19-benchmark list is not enumerated.** "Bar exam, LegalBench, contract analysis" plus 16 unnamed others. Without the list, scores cannot be replicated or audited.
2. **No scoring transparency.** No prompt format, eval harness, model temperature, or n-shot count is published on the page.
3. **No methodology for "arena" component.** "Best Overall: Highest combined arena + benchmark score" — the arena score is referenced but not defined.
4. **Commercial bias risk.** The page is part of a site with header strings "Sell Tokens / Services" — llm-stats appears to operate token-resale, which is a structural reason to overweight high-margin models in rankings. This is a concern, not a confirmed bias.
5. **Pricing inaccuracies in the leaderboard itself** (cross-checked against OpenRouter live catalogue, today):
   - MiniMax M2.1: page says $0.30/$1.20, actual is $0.29/$0.95 (output 21% lower than page claim).
   - Qwen3.6 Plus: page says $0.50/$3.00, actual is $0.325/$1.95 (input 35% lower, output 35% lower than page claim).
   - Qwen3.7 Max: page says $1.25/$3.75, actual matches.
   - Claude Opus 4.7 / 4.8: page says $5.00/$25.00, actual matches.

   Three of five spot-checks accurate; two inflated. Net: pricing data is partially unreliable, treat numbers in your own decisions only from `model_capabilities.yaml`, not from the leaderboard.
6. **"Claude Fable 5" sanity check.** Real on OpenRouter (`anthropic/claude-fable-5`), 1M context, $10/$50 in/out — i.e. **the most expensive Claude variant currently sold, twice the price of Opus 4.8**. The leaderboard ranks it #1 but doesn't surface that price. The naming ("Fable") sits outside Anthropic's standard Opus/Sonnet/Haiku family; I cannot independently confirm release notes from Anthropic's own model documentation in this session. If you plan to rely on Fable 5, fetch the model card directly before committing — not just the leaderboard claim.

### A.4 How to discount it

Treat the leaderboard as **one weak signal among several**, biased toward US-jurisdiction tasks and possibly toward commercial-margin models. Useful as a rough ordering of frontier-capability models. Useless as a substitute for an AU-jurisdiction eval. Where it agrees with other sources (Anthropic / OpenAI release notes, your own evals, the litassist `judge-eval` harness), believe it more. Where it disagrees, believe it less.

### A.5 Independent cross-check on the top-ranked models

- **Claude Opus 4.8**: cross-checked against Anthropic's standard model lineage in the OpenRouter catalogue. Real, 1M ctx, $5/$25, plausible successor to Opus 4.7. No release-note verification possible in this session.
- **Claude Fable 5**: present in catalogue at premium price; outside the standard family naming. **Yellow flag.** Verify the model card before relying on it.
- **Qwen3.7 Max**: present in catalogue, structured_outputs and tools both supported, 1M ctx. Consistent with Alibaba Cloud's published Qwen3 line where 3.5 → 3.6 → 3.7 successively. Plausibly the current Qwen flagship.
- **MiniMax M2.1**: present at $0.29/$0.95, 200K ctx. The leaderboard touts it as "Best Value" and "Best Open Weights", but the actual context window is 200K, not the 1M+ the leaderboard's blurb implies; the larger-context MiniMax M3 ($0.30/$1.20, 1M ctx) is available and is plausibly the better fit if context is the deciding factor.

## D. Three watch-outs specific to legal LLM use

### D.1 Citation hallucination is the failure mode that matters

LitAssist's user-visible quality bar is "the cases you cite must exist and stand for what you say they stand for". UBE multiple-choice scores have zero load-bearing relationship to this property. A model that confidently cites *Smith v Jones [2019] HCA 47* when no such case exists is failing in exactly the way that matters most to a litigation user.

Specific candidates to watch:
- **Qwen3.7 Max / Plus, MiniMax M3, Kimi K2.5**: trained corpus weight on US/Chinese law; AU case-law density is low. High prior for confidently invented AU citations.
- **Claude Fable 5**: no published track record I can verify in this session. Trust no quality claim about it until you have run your own evals.
- **gpt-5.5 vs o3-pro**: same vendor, comparable training; the gap is plausibly modest. Lowest hallucination risk among the TRIAL candidates.

Mitigation: any swap target must be evaluated against the existing `litassist verify --citations` pipeline on a gold set before going to production. The Section E protocol covers this.

### D.2 Jurisdiction sensitivity — US-strong ≠ AU-competent

Bar-exam-strong models project US doctrines onto AU questions. Specific examples worth watching for in an eval:
- **Fourth Amendment leakage**: a US-trained model may reach for US Fourth Amendment reasoning on AU search-and-seizure questions, where the AU position is statutory (LEPRA in NSW, equivalent state Acts elsewhere) and quite different.
- **First Amendment leakage**: AU has no equivalent free-speech protection; implied freedom of political communication (Lange v ABC) is narrower and different.
- **Diversity jurisdiction / federal-state split confusion**: US federalism is not AU federalism. Mis-mapping is common in US-trained models.
- **Bar-exam-trained register**: the UBE essay format is not how AU counsel write; AGLC pinpoint conventions are not US Bluebook conventions.

Watch-out severity per candidate:
- **Qwen, MiniMax, Kimi**: low US bias *and* low AU coverage — likely to invent rather than mis-map. Different failure mode, same severity.
- **Claude Fable 5, Opus 4.8, gpt-5.5**: standard US-leaning frontier models; mis-mapping risk is the dominant failure mode rather than invention.

The existing eval set in `test-scripts/` and the `litassist verify` pipeline already test for many of these. Don't switch off either before a swap.

### D.3 Output-format stability for downstream parsing

LitAssist's pipeline depends on shape stability:
- `extractfacts` must produce the 10-heading YAML structure that `caseplan` / `brainstorm` / `strategy` / `draft` / `barbrief` then read.
- `lookup-irac` must produce Issue / Rule / Application / Conclusion sections.
- `cove-answers` and `cove-questions` produce parseable lists.
- `faithfulness-claims` produces atomic claim enumerations.

"Supports `structured_outputs`" in the OpenRouter catalogue is necessary but not sufficient. Common failure modes when swapping:
- Strict JSON-schema returns syntactically valid output that re-orders or renames keys when the model's underlying training favoured a different order.
- Reasoning models (`thinking_effort: max`) sometimes emit the reasoning trace into the structured output despite the schema saying otherwise.
- Asian-frontier models occasionally inject Chinese-language metadata or punctuation into otherwise English structured outputs, breaking parsers tuned to English.

Mitigation: every TRIAL must run the existing per-command structured-output parser against ≥ 30 outputs from the candidate before any switch. The `enforce_citations`, key-order-preservation, and length-bounding properties all need explicit checks. The repo's existing offline integrity tests cover model presence in capabilities YAML but not output shape — that gap is worth closing in the same PR as any swap.

## E. Suggested A/B-test protocol

A small, concrete eval harness so the swaps can be decided on evidence rather than leaderboard signal.

### E.1 Eval set construction

- **20 real AU case fact patterns** drawn from your existing `examples/` and previous LitAssist runs. Mix of civil, family, and criminal; mix of state and federal; mix of NSW / Vic / Qld / WA / Cth.
- **For each case, 5 representative prompts** per command being evaluated. The exact prompt should be the one the command emits in normal use, not a hand-crafted eval prompt. (The eval should test the production code path, not a parallel one.)
- **Ground truth per prompt**:
  - For commands that produce citations (`lookup`, `extractfacts`, `verify-soundness`): a list of cases that *should* be cited (with Jade.io URLs as verification anchors) and a list of cases that *must not* be cited (because they don't exist or are off-point).
  - For commands that produce structured output (`extractfacts`, `lookup-irac`, `cove-questions`, `faithfulness-claims`): the schema and minimum required keys.
  - For commands that produce free-form output (`draft`, `barbrief`, `counselnotes`): a 5-point rubric (issue identification, statement of facts, applicable law, application, conclusion) scored by your existing `judge-eval` harness, plus AGLC compliance score.

### E.2 Scoring metrics

For each command × candidate model combination, compute:

| Metric | Definition | Pass threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Parse success rate | Fraction of outputs that the production parser accepts on first try | ≥ 95% |
| Citation precision | Fraction of cited cases/statutes that exist in AustLII | ≥ 98% |
| Citation recall | Fraction of expected citations that appeared in the output | ≥ 80% of incumbent's recall |
| AGLC compliance | 0–5 scale, scored by your `judge-eval` rubric | ≥ incumbent − 0.3 |
| Jurisdictional correctness | Was the relevant state/federal law applied? 0 / 1 per prompt | ≥ 95% |
| Refusal rate | Fraction of prompts the model refused | ≤ incumbent + 5pp |
| Cost per 100 prompts | Sum of token cost × OR rate | informational |
| P50 / P95 latency | Wall-clock time per call | informational |

### E.3 Decision rule

**Incumbent wins ties.** Swap to the candidate only if:
1. Citation precision is **≥ incumbent** (this is the hard constraint), and
2. Parse success rate, AGLC compliance, jurisdictional correctness, and recall are all within 5 percentage points of incumbent (down or up), and
3. Cost per 100 prompts is materially lower (≥ 25% reduction is a useful threshold).

If a candidate beats incumbent on (1) decisively but loses on (3), keep incumbent — quality wins. If a candidate is roughly even on (1) and (2) and dramatically cheaper on (3), swap.

### E.4 Implementation sketch

The repo already has most of the machinery:
- `test-scripts/test_quality.py` and `test-scripts/test_unorthodox_models.py` cover real-API model behaviour.
- `judge-eval` is the per-command scoring config.
- `litassist verify --citations` already validates citation existence against AustLII / Google CSE.
- `litassist refresh` regenerates `model_capabilities.yaml` for any new candidate id.

A minimum-viable A/B run for one candidate (e.g. gpt-5.5 → opus-4.8 on `verification`) is:

1. Add `verification-trial` to `model_configs.yaml` with `model: "anthropic/claude-opus-4.8"`.
2. Run `litassist refresh`.
3. Run the existing verification fixtures with `--config verification-trial` and capture outputs.
4. Run `judge-eval` over both incumbent and trial outputs.
5. Compare the metrics table above.
6. If pass: rename `verification-trial → verification`, delete the old. If fail: delete `verification-trial` and move on.

The same shape works for every TRIAL row in Section C. Doing one role per evening keeps the cost of getting it wrong contained.
